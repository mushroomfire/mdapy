    Verification of the DFT¶

    A density functional code is only as useful as it is right. MDAPY's plane-wave DFT is checked in three ways, from the code to the physics:

    1. against its own derivatives and invariances;
    2. against Quantum ESPRESSO 7.6 (pw.x), run on the same input;
    3. against all-electron equations of state of 72 elements, from hydrogen to radon, in four crystal structures, and of six oxides of 49 metals, by the protocol of Bosoni et al. (2024).
    4. against the all-electron equations of state of six magnetic elements with spin, those of the Delta project.

    The first two show that MDAPY solves the equations it means to solve. The last two measure its results against the exact solution of density functional theory with the same functional, where what remains is the error of the pseudopotentials.

    Its own derivatives¶

    The tests check, each time they run:

    Quantity Against Agreement
    forces central differences of the energy of displaced atoms (silicon, aluminium, iron with spin; norm-conserving, ultrasoft and PAW) 1e-7 hartree/bohr
    stress differences of the energy of strained cells whose plane waves keep their Miller indices 5e-8 hartree
    symmetry the same cell on the whole mesh of k-points without symmetry 1e-14 hartree (norm-conserving), 1e-8 (PAW, see below)
    exchange and correlation libxc, point by point, with and without spin 1e-10 of the energy
    eigenvalues the dense matrix of the Hamiltonian diagonalized by NumPy 1e-10 hartree
    PAW one-center terms on the GPU a NumPy implementation of the same equations 1e-11 of each term

    With PAW, the one-center terms are integrated over a set of directions that is not cubic, as in Quantum ESPRESSO; a cell run without symmetry keeps a trace of that, 1e-8 hartree in bcc iron, which the symmetry, averaging the occupations and strengths of the projectors over the operations, removes, as Quantum ESPRESSO's does.

    Quantum ESPRESSO on the same input¶

    Every term of the energy, the eigenvalues, the Fermi level, the forces, the stress and the magnetization agree with pw.x 7.6 on the same cells, pseudopotentials, cutoffs, k-points and FFT grids, for norm-conserving, ultrasoft and PAW pseudopotentials, with and without spin, with and without symmetry. The tests run pw.x each time; the numbers are on the page of the DFT.

    All-electron equations of state¶

    Bosoni et al. computed the equations of state of every element from hydrogen to curium in four cubic structures, FCC, BCC, SC and diamond, and of six oxides of each, with two all-electron codes, WIEN2k and FLEUR, whose results agree with each other closely; their average is the reference (data, CC BY 4.0). They also ran nine pseudopotential codes on the same structures by the same protocol, among them Quantum ESPRESSO with the pseudopotentials used here.

    The protocol¶

    MDAPY follows it as the paper sets it out, on the input of the Quantum ESPRESSO the paper ran:

    • PBE ("pbe_pw") without spin, the valence scalar relativistic;
    • the pseudopotentials of PseudoDojo 0.4, PBE, scalar relativistic, standard, in UPF (van Setten et al. 2018), at the cutoff of their "normal" hint, 18 to 55 hartree, and ecutrho four times that, as the paper's Quantum ESPRESSO took them;
    • the FFT grids Quantum ESPRESSO makes for these cutoffs: of the factors 2, 3 and 5 and, with symmetry, multiples of the denominators of the fractional translations (4 in diamond);
    • Fermi–Dirac smearing of 0.0045 Ry, and the free energy E − TS;
    • seven volumes from 94 % to 106 % of the central volume of the set, in steps of 2 %;
    • a Γ-centered mesh of k-points no more than 0.06 1/Å apart (2π included) along each reciprocal vector at the smallest volume, the same mesh at every volume: from 17 × 17 × 17 to 61 × 61 × 61, 400 to 4000 irreducible k-points with the symmetry of the crystal;
    • the Birch–Murnaghan equation of state fitted as the Delta project fits it, the energy a cubic polynomial in V^(−2/3), for the volume V0, the bulk modulus B0 and its derivative B1.

    Two numbers compare two equations of state. ν is 100 times the length of the relative differences of V0, B0 and B1, the last two weighted by 1/20 and 1/400. ε is the root of the mean squared difference of the two energies over the volumes, aligned at their minima, over the root of the product of their variances. Below 0.1 for ν or 0.06 for ε the paper calls the agreement excellent; below 0.33 or 0.2, good.

    What brings two codes this close is the same input in full: the cutoff the reference took (at the "high" hints, a few hartree higher, the softest structures move by tenths of a meV: ν 0.24 for sodium in diamond, 0.28 for BaO3) and the same FFT grid at every volume, since the exchange and correlation of GGA is integrated on the grid (grids of 50 and 54 points where Quantum ESPRESSO kept 60 made ν 0.8 for neon in diamond).

    ε is computed as the paper defines it; the scripts of the paper take the mean energy of the first curve where the definition has that of the second, which changes ε by about 1e-4 of itself.

    Reproducing it¶

    The script benchmarks/dft_verification.py downloads the structures and the reference data of the paper and the pseudopotentials of PseudoDojo with their hints, runs the volumes of each structure and compares them:

    pip install spglib  # the symmetry of the crystals
python benchmarks/dft_verification.py prepare verification
python benchmarks/dft_verification.py run verification Fe BCC Fe FCC Fe SC Fe Diamond --hint normal
python benchmarks/dft_verification.py run verification Fe XO Fe X2O3 --hint normal  # oxides
python benchmarks/dft_verification.py report verification table.md --hint normal
python benchmarks/dft_verification.py figure verification verification.png unaries --hint normal
python benchmarks/dft_verification.py figure verification oxides.png oxides --hint normal

    The 288 structures of the elements took 36 GPU-hours, mostly on V100 GPUs, 1 to 22 minutes each; the 294 oxides 73 GPU-hours.

    The 56 structures of the lanthanides from cerium to lutetium, for which SSSP takes the atompaw PAW datasets of the Wentzcovitch group with the 4f shell in the valence, lie 0.006 to 1.12 in ν from the paper's Quantum ESPRESSO, 0.20 in the median, and both codes lie far from the all-electron results (ν 3.8 in the median for each). pw.x 7.6 on MDAPY's input decides here too: its equations of state lie within ν 0.0015 of MDAPY's in all 56 (0.0001 in the median), its energies within 0.0006 meV per atom at every volume, and as far from the paper's as MDAPY's.     Magnetic elements¶

    The protocol above is without spin. The Delta project (Lejaeghere et al. 2016) took six elements with their magnetic order: oxygen, chromium and manganese antiferromagnetic (the two molecules of oxygen, the corner and the centre of bcc chromium, and the (001) planes of manganese in its simpler fcc cell opposed), iron, cobalt and nickel ferromagnetic, with WIEN2k as the all-electron reference. MDAPY takes them with spin, the first moments of these orders, and the protocol above otherwise; against pw.x on the same input (the moments, the mesh, the grids, conv_thr 1e-11 Ry) and against WIEN2k, with PseudoDojo at its "normal" hints and with SSSP 1.3 precision (Prandini et al. 2018):

    • The pseudopotentials. Δ is the root of the mean squared difference of two equations of state over ±6 % of the volume, the number of the Delta project.

    Release notes¶

    2.1.0 (not released)¶

    • DislocationResult.density and DislocationResult.densities() give the length of the lines per volume of the box, in all and of each family of Burgers vectors. The page shows how to analyze some atoms only, such as one sublattice of an alloy, as OVITO's option to analyze the selected atoms does: see Some atoms only.
    • DislocationAnalysis(..., low_ca_ratio=True) finds the atoms of hcp of a low c/a, below some 1.15, stretched along their c axis to the ideal c/a, as OVITO's option of the name: a hot crystal is then hcp down to a c/a of some 0.75. See Parameters.
    • DislocationResult.write(path) writes the lines, the crystal clusters and the defect mesh of a dislocation analysis as a crystal analysis (CA) file of OVITO, which OVITO opens, and mdapy.analysis.read_dislocations(path) reads such files back, also those OVITO writes; see CA files.
    • Elastic constants along directions and in a polycrystal: ElasticResult.young_modulus(direction=None, *, average="hill") and ElasticResult.linear_compressibility(direction) along a direction or each of many, ElasticResult.shear_modulus(average="hill", *, normal=None, direction=None) on a plane along a direction, ElasticResult.poisson_ratio(direction=None, transverse=None, *, average="hill") for a stretch and a direction across it, the Young's modulus and the Poisson ratio of a polycrystal without directions, and ElasticResult.universal_anisotropy(). plot.elastic_surface(result, quantity="young_modulus", *, ax=None, extreme=None, points=60, colormap="viridis") draws a quantity along every direction as a surface in 3D, and plot.elastic_section(result, quantity="young_modulus", normal=(0, 0, 1), *, ax=None, extreme=None, points=360, color=None, label=None) along the directions of a plane. See Elastic constants and the example Elastic anisotropy of copper and titanium.
    • quasi_harmonic and thermal_elastic_constants with the quasi-harmonic method raise InvalidParameter when a pair of atoms comes within displacement of the cutoff of the calculator between the volumes or the strained cells. The force constants of NEP and tabulated EAM jump there, and the results jumped with them: with a NEP model, C33 of hcp magnesium at 600 K came out 49 GPa at a strain of 2.5e-3 and 33 GPa at 5e-3, instead of 54 GPa.
    • plot.panel_label labels axes in 3D too; it raised TypeError there.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.ForceConstants(cell, supercell, second) holds the second order force constants of a crystal in a supercell; phonons() gives their Phonons, and write(path, *, full=False) and read_force_constants(path, cell, supercell) write and read phonopy's FORCE_CONSTANTS, compact or full, for any supercell matrix: force constants of phonopy, from VASP for example, give MDAPY's phonons. See Force constants.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.force_constants(cell, frames, *, symmetry=None) fits the second order force constants of a crystal to the forces of displaced supercells from any calculator or from DFT, with the symmetry of the crystal, the exchange of the two atoms and the sum rule exact, and gives their number of free parameters and the relative error of the forces; displaced_supercells(cell, *, supercell, method="finite", displacement=0.01, count=1, seed=None, symmetry=None) makes the supercells, the moves of phonons or random patterns by plus and minus. The atoms of the frames are matched by position, so OUTCAR files of VASP serve as they are.
    • force_constants takes a cutoff: the force constants of the pairs of the crystal no farther apart than it, under every operation of the crystal also in supercells of less symmetry, and zero beyond; ForceConstants.cutoff says which. The page gives how far the frequencies of copper move with the cutoff.
    • force_constants takes a cutoff_third: third order force constants, of three atoms each within it of the others, under every operation of the crystal and every order of the atoms and summing to zero over the third, fitted with the second order from random patterns. ForceConstants holds them as third, (n, nS, nS, 3, 3, 3), with cutoff_third and parameters_third, and takes them as its fourth argument.
    • Phonons.group_velocities(q, *, symmetry=None) gives the group velocity of each mode in Å/ps, from the derivative of the dynamical matrix, with modes of equal frequency set apart along (1, 2, 3) and averaged over the rotations that keep the wave vector, as phonopy does.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.thermal_conductivity(constants, temperatures, *, mesh, sigma=None, symmetry=None) gives the lattice thermal conductivity in the relaxation-time approximation from second and third order force constants, by three-phonon scattering with linear tetrahedra of every rotation of the crystal, or Gaussians of width sigma, on the irreducible wave vectors of a mesh. Its ThermalConductivity holds kappa, the q and weights of the irreducible wave vectors, and the frequencies, group_velocities, gamma, lifetimes and heat_capacities of their modes, the scattering computed in parallel in C++. See Thermal conductivity.
    • Phonons.thermal_displacements(temperatures, *, mesh=20) gives the mean products of the displacements of each atom along each pair of axes, with the zero point motion: the mean square displacements and the Debye–Waller factors.
    • Phonons.gruneisen(q, *, smaller, larger) gives the mode Grüneisen parameters from the phonons of the crystal at a smaller and a larger volume, as phonopy does.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.green_kubo(system, potential, temperature, ...) gives the thermal conductivity from the heat flux of equilibrium molecular dynamics in LAMMPS by the Green–Kubo relation, with the centroid stress of each atom for many-body potentials; green_kubo_from_flux does it with a flux from any dynamics. Its GreenKubo holds the temperature, volume, the times of the correlation, the running integral, kappa, its errors from blocks and the flux. See Green–Kubo conductivity.
    • A new example, Lattice dynamics of solid argon, follows argon from the forces of a few displaced supercells to its thermal conductivity: phonons, the files of phonopy, thermal displacements, Grüneisen parameters, springs along the bonds, third order force constants, and the conductivity of the phonons against that of molecular dynamics.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.bond_stiffness(data, *, cutoff, degree=1) fits springs along and across bonds, of stiffnesses polynomial in the length for each pair of elements in each shell, to the forces of displaced supercells of several crystals, after van de Walle and Ceder. Its BondStiffness holds the pairs, the shells, the degree, the longitudinal and transverse coefficients, the lengths of the bonds fitted and the error, and gives the stiffness of any bond and the force_constants, so the phonons and vibrational free energy, of any arrangement of the elements, such as a special quasirandom structure. See Bond stiffness.
    • The example A collision cascade in tungsten: a primary knock-on atom of 2 keV, a time step that adapts, a border kept at 300 K, and the Frenkel pairs over time; it holds the few lines of 1.x's set_pka.
    • mdapy.fitting, curves and surfaces through data: Spline and GridSpline, cubic splines along a line and on a grid, with derivatives, Spline.integral and the BOUNDARIES at their ends, periodic among them; fit_polynomial and fit_surface, least-squares polynomials of one and two variables, which give a PolynomialFit and a SurfaceFit; fit_curve, functions with parameters fitted by the Levenberg–Marquardt method into a CurveFit, from a Model (its name, function, parameters and guess) or a plain function, with the MODELS exponential, stretched_exponential, gaussian, lorentzian, arrhenius and power_law; and fit_eos, an EquationOfState of one of the FORMS Birch–Murnaghan, Vinet and Murnaghan, with its energy and pressure at any volume. Each fit gives its parameters with their errors and covariance. See Interpolation, Curve fitting and Equations of state.
    • DislocationAnalysis is no longer experimental. Its page lists the limits, general grain boundaries first, and where the results differ from OVITO and why. The stair-rods 1/6<310> and 1/3<110> of fcc have their own families, which were other.
    • melting_point takes a run as the answer only when the mean temperatures of its last two stretches of coexistence agree, within tolerance or twice their statistical error, besides the drift within the last. Before, one stretch could pass while the temperature still rose: for copper from guesses of 1000, 1327 and 1700 K the answers spread by 4 to 17 K, now by 1 to 7 K. error is the statistical error of one run, smaller than that spread.
    • mdapy.transform.orthogonal_cell(system, *, minimal=False, max_search=20, tolerance=1e-6) puts a crystal in a box whose lattice vectors lie along x, y and z, as atomsk's orthogonal cell; see Orthogonal cells.
    • combine fills a column of arrays of more than one dimension, such as the atomic_virials a relaxation leaves, with zeros of their shape for the atoms of the other system; it raised a polars error.
    • StructureFactor of the direct method refuses more than 108 wave vectors, which take 1.6 GB, rather than 109, which took 16 GB.
    • MolecularDynamics gives the time LAMMPS has run in thermo and in the info of frames, also after the time step changed, as with fix dt/reset; it gave the steps times the first time step.
    • System.with_box given lattice vectors keeps the origin and the periodicity of the box; it made the box periodic at the origin, so a slab became periodic.
    • write of a LAMMPS data file is by default in the units the file was read in, as for its atom style; a file in real units with coefficients could not be written back without units="real".
    • build_crystal takes one repeat for every vector, as replicate does, and refuses a or c that are not numbers with InvalidParameter; text raised TypeError.
    • render(color=) takes a component such as "positions.z" or an expression over the columns, as color_coding does, with its color bar in the legend.
    • write_png writes RGB images too, as write_movie takes them.
    • Reading a CIF file warns of partly occupied sites, whose atoms are all kept.
    • element_from_mass recognizes Th, Pa and U, which have standard atomic weights.
    • set_num_threads takes NumPy integers; atomic_numbers("Cu") says to pass a sequence rather than complaining of the symbol u; Topology refuses atom indices that are not whole numbers, which it truncated; Box.from_lengths_angles refuses angles outside 0 to 180 degrees, which gave a mirrored box; Box says when its volume is beyond float64, which it called linearly dependent vectors.
    • RingStatistics stops at once when cancelled, also inside the search from one atom, which for large rings in a dense network takes minutes: rings up to 14 in a random network of coordination 13 stopped after 128 s, now after 0.3 s.
    • CommonNeighborAnalysis with adaptive cutoffs takes OVITO's factor 1.207 for bcc, so that hot bcc atoms at the edge of the cutoff get OVITO's structure; it took (1 + √2)/2.
    • SurfaceMesh gives an atom on two sheets of the surface a vertex on each, and smooths each vertex over the faces about it, as OVITO: the smoothed area of porous solids is OVITO's; it was larger by up to a fifth.
    • PolyhedralTemplateMatching finds the same structures in any unit of length; in metres or in units a billion times larger than Å most structures were lost.
    • RadialDistributionFunction and StructureFactor keep at most 64 partial results in memory; with many elements and bins they took gigabytes.
    • render refuses an outline_width above 100 pixels, which took minutes.
    • Predictions.rmse() and Predictions.table() average the errors of a virial over its six independent components, as GPUMD; the shear components counted twice.
    • check_frames finds an atom too close to its own periodic images in a frame of one atom.
    • io.write writes beside the file and puts the new one in its place when complete: a write that fails leaves a file already there as it was; it removed it before.
    • read_trajectory(..., topology=) keeps the periodicity a frame's file tells (LAMMPS dumps, extended XYZ, MDAPY files), as a LAMMPS data file cannot tell its boundaries: a slab read with its dumps stays a slab. Before, the frames were periodic as the topology, as in OVITO.
    • random_alloy, sqs_alloy and substitute give the atoms they change the mass of their element, and keep types only where each element keeps one, so that a LAMMPS data file written after them tells the elements apart; it gave every atom the old type. add_interstitials gives the atoms it adds the mass and type of their element, and zero charges and velocities, rather than those of the first atom, and keeps the topology.
    • replicate without image flags joins a bond, angle, dihedral or improper across a periodic boundary to the next copy, as LAMMPS replicate ... bond/periodic; it kept it in one copy, across the box.
    • from_ase takes atoms without a cell, such as a molecule, and with cell vectors of zero along open directions, such as a slab without vacuum; to_ase_calculator gives them energies and forces, without stress.
    • combine gives the atoms of a system without masses or types those of their elements, and numbers the atoms of a system without ids from 1; it gave zero masses, which were refused, and id 0.
    • add_graphene takes a polycrystal with more columns than build_polycrystal gives, such as velocities, types or masses, and gives the carbon atoms those of combine; it raised ColumnNotFoundError.
    • create_bonds makes one bond per pair of atoms in a small box, where several images of a pair are close.
    • EntropyFingerprint counts every neighbor within the cutoff: those farther than about 40 sigma, beyond 7.9 Å with the default sigma=0.2, added nothing before, as their Gaussians underflowed.
    • DislocationAnalysis: a line traced in a sliver of crystal, such as the layer of crystal inside an extrinsic fault, gets its Burgers vector in the largest crystal cluster related to it, whose frame is fitted to many atoms; its spatial vector was off by up to 0.5 Å. Where one cluster holds two grains, the spatial Burgers vector of a line follows the lattice about it, not the mean orientation of the cluster; it was off by half the angle between the grains. Elsewhere the results are unchanged.
    • DislocationAnalysis and SurfaceMesh analyze every crystal: perfect fcc, hcp and diamond crystals from some 15 000 atoms on, built systems and hot crystals were refused at random, more often the larger they were, with a message to replicate the system. The tessellation under them left four atoms nearly on one sphere with an atom's Voronoi cell undecided; such atoms are now settled by their order, alike in every cell.
    • The normals of DislocationResult.defect_mesh point into the crystal, as the documentation says; the docstring said out of it.
    • insert_dislocation without elastic: an edge dislocation no longer leaves a fault from its core to the surface when a plane of atoms lies just below the plane of cut. The displacement lifted such atoms across the plane, and they were moved back as if they had been above it, as atomsk does; above and below are now where the atoms were.
    • PolyhedralTemplateMatching without a neighbor list finds the 18 nearest neighbors of each atom as it needs them, rather than in a list first: 2 048 000 atoms of fcc copper take 293 MB beyond the system rather than 752 MB, in the same time, and its progress counts once from start to end.
    • Box.minimum_image gives the shortest vector in boxes of any tilt; with tilts beyond half a box length it could give a longer image.
    • Box.wrap raises InvalidParameter for atoms so many boxes away that their image flags do not fit in int64; it gave wrong flags.
    • Conditions such as "element == 'cu'" refuse texts that are not chemical symbols; they selected nothing. System names such symbols when it refuses them.
    • The progress of EAM and NEP counts once from start to end over their passes; it started again at each pass.

    2.0.0¶

    MDAPY 2.0, written anew, in its first stable release: from here on Compatibility holds, and code written for 2.0.0 keeps working, with the same results, in every later 2.x release. What 2.0 holds is in the notes of 2.0.0a1 below and of each release after it; From MDAPY 1.x gives the way from 1.x. The API is that of 2.0.0rc1, with the options of plot.style below. Since then:

    • pip install mdapy installs 2.0, without --pre; pip install "mdapy<2" keeps 1.x.
    • Installation names the systems officially supported, those with built packages on PyPI; on others, such as macOS on an Intel processor, MDAPY built from source should work, untested.
    • plot.style(*, font_size=None, line_width=None, colors=None, settings=None) changes the size of the text, the width of the lines and the colors they take in turn without matplotlib's names, and takes any other setting of matplotlib by its name; Figures with MDAPY shows how.
    • A fix of one's own, added with MolecularDynamics.command, works in run as in one run of LAMMPS: the setup of run now knows where the run ends, and a fix deform stretched nothing before, as fixes that plan the whole run at their setup planned one of 0 steps. Molecular dynamics through LAMMPS stretches copper in this way.
    • The documentation has versions: latest, the newest release, which readers see by default, each release by its minor version, and dev, the code of master. A menu at the top switches between them; links without a version, as before, go to latest.
    • Extended XYZ files with both force and forces, as when one program adds its own to a file of another, are read: a property named as its column, here forces, gives it, and force keeps its name; they gave an error before. The same holds for charge beside initial_charges.
    • A LAMMPS dump of several frames written from elements, without a type column or elements=, gives each element one type in every frame, in the order the elements first appear; the types followed the order of the atoms of each frame, so an element could change its type between frames.
    • Pictures of large systems seen from far away have no rings: the intersections of the rays with atoms and bonds keep their digits however far the camera is, and the rays of shadows and occlusion leave a surface by more the farther it is from the camera. The rings showed on the GPU above some hundred Å from the eye, more faintly on the processor.

    2.0.0rc1¶

    The release candidate of MDAPY 2.0: its API is that of 2.0.0, and a test keeps it. Until 2.0.0 only fixes follow.

    • Compatibility says what 2.x promises from 2.0.0 on: code that works keeps working, the API changes only with 3.0, a replaced name warns before it goes, and a result changes only to fix a bug, named here.
    • Built packages for Linux on ARM (aarch64), tested like the others.
    • Python 3.15 is tested and supported.
    • The documentation is published from the tag of each release, so that it describes the version pip installs.
    • CITATION.cff gives GitHub's "Cite this repository" the paper to cite.
    • Papers citing MDAPY, by year.
    • From MDAPY 1.x gives the 2.0 way of each common 1.x call.
    • The API of 2.0.0 is fixed, and a test keeps it: a name that goes away or a parameter that changes fails it, and so does an addition these notes do not name. Before that, one name for each concept and options given by name only. From 2.0.0b1:
      • cutoff for every cutoff radius: RadialDistributionFunction(cutoff) and StructureFactor(..., cutoff=) (were r_max), SOAP(species, cutoff) (was r_cut); SOAP.reach is the reach of its neighbors (was SOAP.cutoff).
      • BondLengthDistribution(cutoff=None, bins=100) in the order of BondAngleDistribution; BondLengthResult.bond_lengths (was lengths).
      • sizes of GrainResult and CrystalClusters (were size), as of the other results; TimeAverageResult.num_frames and thermal_phonons(..., num_frames=) (were frames); MolecularDynamics.run(..., save_every=) (was frames), as minimize.
      • atom_colors(..., colormap=) (was colormap_name), atom_radii(system, kind=) (was radius), GrainResult.atom_colors(*, ungrouped=) (was none).
      • melting_point(..., num_atoms=) and check_calculator(..., num_atoms=) (were atoms), principal_components(points, count=) (was n), read_gpumd(directory) (was folder), EAM.average(fractions) (was concentrations), as random_alloy; SpatialBinning(column, bins) (was property), as Histogram.
      • Given by name only: scale_positions of System.with_box, seed of random_rotations, partial and core of Dislocation, the options of color_coding and of SOAP, atoms of CoordinationPolyhedra.compute and of trajectory_lines, by of Predictions.worst, tolerance and candidates of element_from_mass, name of freeze, mesh of Phonons.thermal, formats of plot.save.
      • One path for each name: mdapy.NeighborList (no longer in mdapy.neighbor); mdapy.core holds LammpsBox and Result only, the others are at the top level.
    • Plain XYZ files read: a comment line without = is free text, kept as info["comment"], and each atom line then holds an element symbol and x, y, z.
    • Every NaN is written as nan in text files; some systems wrote -nan(ind) or nan(snan), which no reader takes back.
    • An atom count too large for 64 bits in a damaged header gives a FileFormatError.
    • Damaged files of every format are tested to give an error of MDAPY, and the text kernels are fuzzed in CI.

    2.0.0b1¶

    The beta of MDAPY 2.0: every feature planned for 2.0 is in, and until 2.0.0 only documentation and bug fixes follow.

    • render puts its legend in a column of its own at the left of the picture and frames the scene beside it, so that the legend never covers the atoms or the box. See Rendering.
    • render(line_color=...) takes a color for each line, such as one for each atom whose path trajectory_lines gives.
    • The ninth of LABEL_COLORS is brown: it was the orange of the first.
    • Tests run with the lowest NumPy and polars that MDAPY accepts, and every week with their newest releases, the nightly wheels of NumPy and the pre-releases of polars.
    • render and Scene.render no longer take aperture and focal_distance: the depth of field they gave only blurred a picture, and was easily set wrong.

    Documentation:

    • The README shows the examples as a gallery of pictures, each linked to its page.
    • Polyhedral template matching shows every structure and every chemical ordering it knows, the RMSD and orientations; Voronoi analysis draws cells as voro++ does; Space group explains the point group with its axes and stereograms; Slip systems has the pyramidal systems of hcp; Frozen values follows the atoms of a crystal into its melt.
    • The pictures of stacking faults at finite temperature, of phonons at finite temperature and of phase diagrams come with the code that draws them, and every picture with a legend has it beside the scene.
    • The CrCoNi example compares the elastic constants of molecular dynamics, run with pair_style nep/kk on a GPU, with the quasi-harmonic ones and the moduli of both with measurements.
    • Special quasirandom structures plot their Warren–Cowley parameters shell by shell against those of a random alloy.
    • The performance pages show for each case how many times faster MDAPY is at every size, the largest gains on top. The analyses are measured again with 2.0.0b1, Steinhardt parameters over Voronoi faces now at 2 million atoms too; the dislocation analysis, still experimental, is left out.
    • Reporting a problem says which form of an issue to choose.

    2.0.0a2¶

    • find_overlapping and build_polycrystal delete as few atoms as they can: the atoms that overlap the fewest are kept first. find_overlapping used to delete the later atom of every pair, also when the earlier one was deleted too. See Overlapping atoms.
    • build_polycrystal keeps molecules whole when the unit has a molecule column: a molecule belongs to the grain of its center, those too close go whole, and the topology is kept. It also keeps the other columns of the unit, such as charges. find_overlapping has an option molecules. See A molecular polycrystal.
    • plot.warren_cowley draws the Warren–Cowley parameters as a matrix with its values written in, as MDAPY 1.x did. See Warren–Cowley parameters.
    • build_polycrystal places only the atoms of each grain, on all threads, and gives the same atoms in the same order as before: 2 million atoms of copper in 20 grains, with min_distance 0.01 Å, take 0.017 s instead of 1.9 s on 10 threads, and the process peaks at 182 MB instead of 1043 MB. See Building structures.
    • build_crystal runs on all threads and gives the same atoms in the same order: nearly 10 million atoms of copper take 0.017 s instead of 0.25 s on 10 threads, and the build takes 300 MB instead of 750 MB.
    • Box.wrap runs on all threads.
    • Gzipped files are written on all threads, as one stream of blocks that every program reads, no larger than before: a LAMMPS dump of 2 million atoms takes 0.7 s instead of 4.4 s on 10 threads. Use .gz to compress: the other formats compress on one thread. See Compressed files.
    • LAMMPS data files are read and written about as fast as dumps: their atoms are no longer sorted to check their IDs, and what depends on the type is found once for each type. On 40 threads, 10 million atoms take 0.15 s instead of 0.62 s to read and 0.35 s instead of 0.52 s to write.
    • Text files are written and read faster, with less memory and the same text and values: on 10 threads, a LAMMPS dump of 10 million atoms and 5 columns takes 0.11 s instead of 0.30 s to write and 0.12 s instead of 0.37 s to read. A file on disk takes the text straight from the threads that format it, while they format the next part, and the lines of a file are found on all threads.

    2.0.0a1¶

    MDAPY 2.0 is a complete rewrite and is not compatible with MDAPY 1.x. The name is written MDAPY, with mdapy for the package, the imports and the commands, and it has a new logo.

    The top level of mdapy holds the foundations: System, Box, Topology, NeighborList, the threads, the versions and the modules. Everything else comes from its module, as mdapy.io.read or from mdapy.build import build_crystal.

    Columns of results are named by their method and options, as cna_structure, ptm_rmsd or q6_average_voronoi, so that several analyses stay side by side; with_columns(near=result) gives a prefix, and two values for a column in one call are an error.

    Structures¶

    • mdapy.System: one atomic configuration with a polars table of per-atom properties, a box, global information and an optional topology. Systems are immutable: with_* methods return new systems that share the columns they do not change and check only the ones they do; arrays given to a system are copied, its table is handed out as a copy, it takes no new attributes, and copy.deepcopy returns the system itself; systems, boxes, topologies and triangle meshes pickle, for other processes. Every column, the positions too, is read by name and changed with with_columns; the positions are an (N, 3) array column read without copying, system["positions"]. A summary of a few lines shows the atoms, the box, the columns, the global information and the topology. select, drop and rename choose, remove and rename columns as polars does, and with_info({}) clears the global information. See Systems and Changing a system.
    • System.filter, System.gather and System.sort keep atoms by a condition, take them by index and order them, as polars does with rows. Conditions are Python expressions over the columns, such as "positions.x**2 + positions.y**2 < 25 and element == 'Cu'", with the components of array columns by name or index, comparisons, logic, membership, arithmetic and common functions; keywords, masks and polars expressions work too. System.where gives the atoms of the same conditions as a boolean mask. system["positions.z"] gives the values of a column, a component or such an expression as a read-only NumPy array, without a copy for columns and components. See Selecting and ordering atoms.
    • mdapy.Topology: bonds, angles, dihedrals and impropers with types and labels, kept consistent when atoms are selected or reordered. See Topology.
    • mdapy.Box: immutable simulation box with lattice vectors, origin and periodicity; fractional coordinates, wrapping with image flags, exact minimum images, and conversion to LAMMPS restricted triclinic form. See Simulation box.

    Reading and writing¶

    • mdapy.io.read and mdapy.io.write read one frame and write one or more frames, finding the format from the file name. Files compressed with gzip, bzip2, xz or zstd (Python 3.14) are read and written transparently. mdapy.io.formats and mdapy.io.detect_format list and identify formats. See Reading and writing files.
    • Extended XYZ with the conventions of ASE and GPUMD: velocities from vel (Å/fs) or ASE momenta, stress in GPa, any per-atom column and any global value, written so that they read back unchanged. See Extended XYZ.
    • LAMMPS data files of the atom styles atomic, charge, bond, angle, molecular and full, following LAMMPS stable_30Sep2026: type labels, masses with element guessing, bonds to impropers as the topology, force-field coefficients kept unchanged, image flags, velocities, metal and real units, and restricted and general triclinic boxes. See LAMMPS data.
    • LAMMPS text dumps of the atom and custom styles, with any number of frames: units and time items, orthogonal, triclinic and general triclinic boxes, scaled and unwrapped coordinates, elements and type labels, and every other column, array columns such as c_stress[1] to c_stress[6] kept together. See LAMMPS dump.
    • VASP POSCAR and CONTCAR: scaling factors, POTCAR labels, selective dynamics as the movable column, Direct and Cartesian coordinates, and the velocity block of a CONTCAR. See VASP POSCAR.
    • The MDAPY file format .mdapy: a ZIP archive of Parquet tables that keeps every column with its type, the box, the information and the topology of each frame, a table of frames with typed columns of the information, and recovery of files whose writing stopped early. See MDAPY files.
    • mdapy.io.read_trajectory opens the frames of any file lazily: frames are found and read when used, slices share the open file, and iteration starts at once; also the frames of several files, given as a pattern such as dump.*.lammpstrj or a list, each in the format of its name, and frames that move the atoms of a topology read from a data file, matched by id, with its bonds, charges and molecules, as OVITO's load trajectory; Trajectory.map gives the frames through a function, each computed when it is used, for the analyses of trajectories. See Trajectories. Trajectory.file_of gives the file that holds a frame.
    • Reading CIF files, crystals expanded by their symmetry operations as pymatgen, ASE and OVITO read them, and PDB files, with atom names, residues, models as frames and the bonds of CONECT; VASP OUTCAR files, one frame per ionic step with the forces, the free energy TOTEN, the virial and whether the step converged, as ASE reads them. See CIF, PDB and VASP OUTCAR.
    • Damaged files give a FileFormatError in every format: bytes that are not text, a .mdapy file cut or changed anywhere, a file without atoms that read as an empty system before.

    Neighbor search¶

    • mdapy.NeighborList.build(system, cutoff) and NeighborList.build(system, num_neighbors=k): every pair within a cutoff, or the k nearest neighbors of each atom, in any box, with periodic images enumerated so that small boxes work, in CSR form with int8 image shifts (15 bytes per pair); within and sorted derive lists without a new search. Distances are computed by one formula in both, nearest neighbors are ordered exactly with ties by atom and image, and results are the same for any number of threads. A k-d tree finds nearest neighbors in clusters and slabs as fast as in crystals. A list serves every system with the same positions in the same box (fits), so it outlives with_columns. A system whose atoms fill a tiny part of the space they span, which would take hours, is refused with an error. See Neighbor lists.
    • mdapy.neighbor.expand_selection: a selection grown by the atoms within a cutoff, the nearest neighbors or the bonded atoms of the selected ones, step by step, as OVITO's expand selection. See Expanding a selection.
    • mdapy.neighbor.find_overlapping: the atoms closer than a distance to another, the later atom of each such pair so that deleting them leaves none that close, or all of them, as OVITO's find overlapping particles. See Overlapping atoms.
    • Analyses given a list read it where it is, without a copy: those of the k nearest neighbors put the k nearest pairs of each atom first in the list itself, once, in parallel; those within a cutoff read the pairs closer than theirs and stop at the first sorted pair beyond it. One list within 5 Å serves every structure analysis of a metal; threads may share a list. A list keeps 15 bytes per pair: i and vectors are computed when asked for. See Sharing neighbor lists.
    • Atoms far apart, up to 1e9 box lengths: a search along a direction without periods puts its cells over the body of the atoms, so that an atom that flew off leaves the others as fast as before (0.03 s, not 12 s, for 200 000 atoms), and the number of cells no longer overflows, which could crash the process.
    • A list of Voronoi neighbors finds its distances in parallel and, in a periodic box, keeps the arrays of the cells without a copy: Steinhardt parameters over Voronoi faces take a quarter less time and 40 % less memory.

    Calculators¶

    • mdapy.calculators.Calculator, the interface of every calculator, and CalculatorResult with energies, forces, virials and stress. mdapy.calculators.testing.check_calculator tests any implementation. See Calculators.
    • mdapy.calculators.EAM for potentials in the LAMMPS eam/alloy format, agreeing with LAMMPS.
    • mdapy.calculators.NEP for neuroevolution potentials (nep4 and nep4_zbl files of GPUMD) with every form of ZBL and per-element cutoffs, agreeing with NEP_CPU in double precision; NEP.descriptors gives the descriptors.
    • EAM.write writes potentials in the LAMMPS eam/alloy format; EAM.zhou builds the potentials of Zhou, Johnson and Wadley for 17 elements, as the LAMMPS database tool tabulates them; EAM.average gives the average-atom potential of a random alloy.
    • mdapy.calculators.LAMMPS makes any interaction of LAMMPS a calculator, through the LAMMPS Python module.
    • mdapy.calculators.VASP runs VASP for each calculation with a command, as in the job script of a cluster: it writes the inputs, keeps the wave functions between calls and reads the OUTCAR. CalculationFailed reports a run that stopped. See VASP.

    Simulation¶

    • mdapy.simulation.minimize: FIRE 2.0 minimization of the positions, step for step as LAMMPS min_style fire, and optionally of the box (cell="isotropic", "axes" or "full", at a pressure) to the minimum of LAMMPS fix box/relax; keeps a frame every save_every steps when asked, with the virial and the stress of each. Its steps use no BLAS, whose threads took the cores from the kernels under OpenBLAS (0.5 s against 3.5 s for 4000 atoms on 16 threads). See Energy minimization.
    • mdapy.simulation.free_energy: the free energy of solids and liquids per atom from molecular dynamics in LAMMPS, by switching to springs or to the Uhlenbeck-Ford model and following the enthalpy to other temperatures; crossing gives where two phases meet. See Free energy.
    • mdapy.simulation.melting_point: the melting point from a first guess, by the two-phase method. MolecularDynamics.run takes the ensemble "nph". See Melting point.
    • mdapy.simulation.binary_phase_diagram and plot.phase_diagram: the phase diagram of two elements from the free energies of their phases. See Phase diagrams.
    • mdapy.simulation.pressure_temperature_diagram and plot.pressure_temperature: the phase diagram of one element against pressure and temperature, from the free energies of its phases at each pressure. See Pressure-temperature diagrams.
    • mdapy.simulation.stacking_fault_free_energy: the free energy of a stacking fault at each temperature, from two crystals in one box. See Stacking faults at finite temperature.
    • slip: an atom outside the box moves as its image in the box does; a layer of a relaxed crystal at the bottom of the box was split by rounding, and gave a stacking fault energy far too large.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.elastic_constants: the (6, 6) elastic constants from the stress under small strains, with relaxed positions, and Voigt, Reuss and Hill moduli. See Elastic constants.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.phonons: harmonic phonons from the forces on displaced atoms with any calculator, the force constants made symmetric and free of translations; frequencies and polarization vectors at any wave vector, band structures along standard or given paths, densities of states by the tetrahedron method or Gaussians, projected on the atoms, and the free energy, energy, entropy and heat capacity of the vibrations; the same dynamical matrix as LAMMPS. primitive_cell finds the smallest cell of a crystal; supercells as integer matrices, by default the one nearest a cube; standard paths of any crystal with seekpath, and branches followed through crossings; imaginary frequencies in the density of states; band structures written and read as phonopy's band.dat; plot.band_structure and plot.phonon_dos. units.PLANCK. See Phonons.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.quasi_harmonic: the quasi-harmonic approximation from phonons at several volumes: the equilibrium volume, Gibbs energy, entropy, bulk modulus, thermal expansion and heat capacity at constant pressure against temperature, at any pressure, the shape of the box relaxed at each volume. See Quasi-harmonic approximation.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.thermal_elastic_constants: isothermal and adiabatic elastic constants at finite temperature, quasi-harmonic or quasi-static. See Elastic constants at finite temperature.
    • Sheets: a box periodic along two vectors in the x-y plane makes a system a two-dimensional material. elastic_constants gives its constants in N/m as a SheetElasticResult, with Young's, shear and area moduli and Poisson's ratios; phonons takes supercells and meshes in the plane and band paths through it; primitive_cell takes it too; MolecularDynamics.run controls the pressure in the plane with barostat="xy", and runs a flat sheet, which LAMMPS refused when its height shrank to nothing. See Sheets.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.thermal_phonons and phonons_from_frames: phonons at a temperature, from the forces on atoms in molecular dynamics, by the force constants that best give them. See Phonons at finite temperature.
    • mdapy.lattice_dynamics.md_elastic_constants: isothermal elastic constants at a temperature from the mean stress of molecular dynamics in LAMMPS under small strains, with standard errors from block averages. See Elastic constants from molecular dynamics.
    • Symmetry with spglib (optional, the extra symmetry): phonons, quasi_harmonic, elastic_constants and thermal_elastic_constants skip the displacements and strains that the symmetry of the crystal relates to others, by default whenever spglib is installed: one computation of forces instead of 6 for the primitive cell of fcc, 12 of the 73 strains of the elastic constants of a cubic crystal at finite temperature. The elastic constants take the rotations alone, in milliseconds also for cells of hundreds of atoms. See Space group.

    • mdapy.simulation.MolecularDynamics: molecular dynamics in LAMMPS with the interaction of a LAMMPS calculator: random velocities, runs at constant energy, temperature or pressure with ramps, the thermodynamic state as a polars table, frames and the final system back in the orientation of the box, any LAMMPS command in between; the same trajectory as the same LAMMPS input. See Molecular dynamics.

    • mdapy.simulation.vacancy_formation_energy, surface_energy and stacking_fault_energy (the generalized stacking fault energy, relaxed along the normal), agreeing with LAMMPS; minimize keeps atoms still along the directions where a movable column is False. See Defect energies.

    Analyses¶

    • Analyses are classes whose compute(system) returns an immutable result; System.with_columns(result) adds its per-atom values. See Analyses.
    • Structure identification in mdapy.identify: CommonNeighborAnalysis, adaptive or with a fixed cutoff, and AcklandJonesAnalysis, agreeing atom by atom with LAMMPS, for fcc, hcp, bcc and icosahedral atoms; DiamondStructure for cubic and hexagonal diamond and their first and second neighbors, as OVITO; ChillPlus for ice, hydrates and liquid water; and the centro-symmetry and common neighbor parameters.
    • ChemicalSpecies: molecules and their Hill formulas by the bond rule of Jmol (covalent radii, the default) or of VMD and OVITO (van der Waals radii), radii given by hand or bond lengths per pair of elements, and the number of each along a trajectory; the same molecules as ASE and as OVITO. mdapy.elements has the covalent and van der Waals radii, which pictures now read from it.
    • SpaceGroup: the space group of a crystal, its symmetry operations and equivalent atoms, with spglib, for cells of up to 2000 atoms.
    • PolyhedralTemplateMatching: the structure, orientation, elastic deformation and chemical ordering of each atom, for nine structures, implemented by MDAPY itself; the same as OVITO atom by atom.
    • PlanarFaults: intrinsic and multilayer stacking faults and coherent twin boundaries of fcc crystals from the hcp-like atoms of polyhedral template matching, and their areas; the same as OVITO's Identify fcc planar faults atom by atom; optionally extrinsic stacking faults too.
    • GrainSegmentation: the grains of a polycrystal from the lattice orientations of polyhedral template matching, by node pair sampling with an automatic threshold or along a minimum spanning tree, with stacking faults and twin boundaries joined to the grains around them and orphan atoms adopted; the grain of each atom, and the size, structure, mean orientation, center of mass and radii of each grain, the same as OVITO's Grain segmentation atom by atom.
    • Distributions and order parameters in mdapy.analysis, agreeing with OVITO where it has them: the RadialDistributionFunction and its partial functions; CoordinationNumber with cutoffs per pair of elements; the StructureFactor, exact over the wave vectors of the box or by the Debye method, with Faber–Ziman partial factors; the SpatialCorrelation function of two properties of the atoms, by fast Fourier transforms on a grid and by a direct sum over neighbors, the same as OVITO's spatial correlation function; the BondAngleDistribution for each triplet of elements; the BondLengthDistribution of the bonds of a topology, in total and for each pair of elements; RingStatistics, the shortest-path rings of a network of bonds up to 36 atoms, without the paths around the periodic box, drawn as polygons; WarrenCowley short-range order, over shells of neighbors by distance or by number; SteinhardtParameters up to degree 20, optionally averaged, and with solid=True the solid-like bonds and atoms of ten Wolde, Ruiz-Montero and Frenkel, as freud's SolidLiquid (MDAPY 1.x identify_liquid); the EntropyFingerprint; and the VoronoiAnalysis, computed by MDAPY's own code, with the volumes and coordination of the cells and, when asked for, their faces and Voronoi indices (faces=True), which otherwise are not kept, the face and edge thresholds of OVITO, boxes that are not periodic, and the shapes of the cells as polyhedra; NeighborList.build(system, voronoi=True) for the atoms that share a face, and Steinhardt parameters over them, optionally weighted by the face areas.
    • Dynamics in mdapy.analysis: the MeanSquaredDisplacement of a trajectory, over every time origin or from the first frame, agreeing with LAMMPS and OVITO; the LindemannIndex of each atom and of the frames so far; both match the atoms of the frames by their ids and follow them through the periodic boundaries by their image flags or by the nearest image, unless the positions are already unwrapped. The AtomicTemperature of each atom and its neighbors, relative to their center of mass. TimeAverage: the mean of a column of the atoms, of the box or of anything computed from a frame over a trajectory, the atoms matched by id and their positions unwrapped, as OVITO's time averaging. trajectory_lines: the paths of the atoms, unwrapped by their image flags or the nearest image, drawn by render(lines=...). mdapy.transform.remove_drift: the frames of a trajectory moved to keep the center of mass of all the atoms or of some where it is in a reference frame, read when they are used, as OVITO's center of mass correction. TimeSeries: a column reduced to one number, or anything computed, in every frame, as OVITO's time series; FrameDifference, the change of a column of each atom from a reference frame; mdapy.transform.freeze, the values of a column in a reference frame given to the atoms of another; and mdapy.transform.smooth_trajectory, the positions and other real numbers averaged over a window of frames; each as OVITO's modifier of the same name, the atoms matched by id.
    • Statistics of a column in mdapy.analysis, agreeing with OVITO: Histogram, the counts of the values of a column, a component or an expression in bins, of all atoms or of a mask; reduce, one number from them: mean, sum, extremes, median, variance, standard deviation, the count of those not zero, or the atom of the lowest or highest, NaN values left out.
    • Defects and deformation in mdapy.analysis, agreeing with OVITO: ClusterAnalysis for clusters of bonded atoms and whether they span the box, and on request their unwrapped positions, centers of mass and gyration tensors; AtomicDisplacement, the displacement of each atom from a reference matched by id, with the minimum image and the affine mapping of OVITO's displacement vectors; SpatialBinning, a property in bins along one to three box vectors, its mean, sum, density, minimum or maximum, the values of OVITO's spatial binning; the isosurface of a grid of such values, by marching tetrahedra, across the periodic boundaries, and its cells as colors on the faces of the box by mdapy.visualization.voxel_faces; WignerSeitz for vacancies and interstitials, the sites of the reference kept in a k-d tree for every configuration; and AtomicStrain, the von Mises and volumetric strains against a reference, its atoms matched by id, and on request the Green–Lagrange strain tensor and \(D^2_\text{min}\). As in OVITO, the results that cost more are computed only when asked for.
    • ElasticStrain in mdapy.analysis: the elastic strain of each atom of an fcc, hcp, bcc or diamond crystal against the ideal crystal of given lattice constants, in the spatial frame, with the structures of the dislocation analysis; the same as OVITO's elastic strain calculation atom by atom.
    • SurfaceMesh in mdapy.analysis: the surface of the solid of atoms by the alpha-shape method of OVITO's Construct surface mesh, from a Delaunay tessellation of MDAPY's own, with its area, the volume of the solid, the regions of solid and empty space and the voids among them, the atoms on the surface, and Taubin's smoothing; the same areas, volumes and atoms as OVITO. The surface is a mdapy.analysis.TriangleMesh, a mesh of triangles across periodic boundaries.
    • DislocationAnalysis in mdapy.analysis, experimental: the dislocation lines of fcc, hcp, bcc, cubic and hexagonal diamond crystals by OVITO's dislocation extraction algorithm, written anew: Burgers vectors in the lattice frame and in space, partials of stacking faults, junctions, loops, the defect mesh around grain boundaries and surfaces; the same structures atom by atom as OVITO, the same lines and lengths within a per cent on straight dislocations and loops, within several per cent in networks and at grain boundaries.

    Structure builder¶

    • mdapy.build.build_crystal: perfect crystals of fifteen structures, cubic and hexagonal, of one element or ordered on their sublattices, along any crystal directions, with the shortest period along each; the same atoms as atomsk, ordered plane by plane without sorting them (2 million atoms in some 0.05 s). See Crystals.
    • mdapy.build.random_alloy: elements at random in given fractions with exact counts. See Random alloys.
    • mdapy.build.sqs_alloy: special quasirandom structures, the pairs of the nearest shells those of a random alloy. See SQS.
    • Defects in mdapy.build: add_vacancies, add_interstitials and substitute; slip for stacking faults; add_vacuum for surfaces; insert_dislocation, the isotropic screw and edge dislocations of atomsk, and edge_dislocation of two crystals. See Point defects and Dislocations.
    • Dislocations in anisotropic elasticity in mdapy.build: insert_dislocation(..., elastic=), the displacements of Stroh, the same as atomsk with an elastic tensor; dislocation_cylinder, a cylinder around a dislocation with a fixed outer shell (movable), and dislocation_quadrupole, a periodic quadrupole in a box tilted by the plastic strain of the dipole; DISLOCATIONS, the common dislocations of bcc, fcc and diamond (glide set), or a Dislocation of your own; fcc and diamond ones split into Shockley partials around an intrinsic stacking fault. Burgers vectors as the dislocation analysis and OVITO find them, the opposite of Hirth and Lothe's (and atomsk's). See Dislocations.
    • mdapy.build.add_graphene: graphene on the grain boundaries of a polycrystal, one or more AB-stacked layers centered on each face between two grains, each face once, in a random orientation, cut to the face; a fraction of the boundary area covered by whole faces; the atoms of the grains too close to the carbon removed. build_polycrystal keeps the seeds in info["seeds"]. See Graphene on grain boundaries.
    • mdapy.build.build_polycrystal: Voronoi grains of any periodic unit, columnar when the box is thin, twin_lamellae for twinned grains, rotations_about for the turns of columns, and gradient_seeds for grains whose size changes along one direction, from one side or both. See Polycrystals, which also builds grains of graphene, and Gamma surfaces.
    • mdapy.transform.replicate copies a system along its periodic directions as LAMMPS replicate does, with the ids, molecules and topology of each copy and molecules kept whole through the image flags; wrap and unwrap move atoms into the box or to their unwrapped positions. See Periodic copies and images.
    • mdapy.transform.slice: the atoms on one side of any plane or in a slab about it, as the Slice of OVITO: a normal in Cartesian coordinates or as Miller indices of the box, at a distance or through a point, acting on all atoms or some; slice_mask the same as a mask. See Slices.
    • mdapy.transform.affine: moves, rotates, shears or scales the atoms, their velocities, forces and displacements, and the box, all atoms or a selection, as OVITO's affine transformation modifier. See Affine transformation.
    • mdapy.transform.combine: the atoms of a second system added to a first, in its box, as OVITO's combine datasets: the columns of either, ids numbered on, molecules and topology terms offset. See Combining systems.
    • mdapy.transform.create_bonds: bonds in the topology between the atoms closer than a cutoff, or covalent bonds, as OVITO's create bonds; drawn by render(bonds="topology"). See Creating bonds.

    MLIP¶

    • Training sets from VASP: the ionic steps of OUTCAR files, read as trajectories with energies, forces, virials and convergence, written to extended XYZ. See Training sets from VASP.
    • mdapy.mlip.SOAP: the smooth overlap of atomic positions of each atom, the same values as DScribe's SOAP with Gaussian radial functions, in C++, for any structures whatever made them. See SOAP descriptors.
    • mdapy.mlip.structure_descriptors, farthest_point_sampling and principal_components: frames chosen far from a training set and from each other in the space of NEP descriptors, and their projection on a plane. See Choosing frames.
    • mdapy.mlip.perturb: copies of a structure with atoms displaced and the box strained at random. See Perturbed structures.
    • mdapy.mlip.predict and read_gpumd: errors of any model against the labels of a training set, frame by frame, or read from the outputs of NEP training in GPUMD and of torchnep. See Errors of a model.
    • mdapy.mlip.committee and novelty: the spread of the predictions of several models and the distance from a training set in the space of descriptors, to find frames worth labeling. See Uncertainty and novelty.
    • mdapy.mlip.check_frames: atoms too close, net forces and missing labels in a training set. See Checking frames.
    • Training sets of many small frames: predict, committee and structure_descriptors compute NEP models and SOAP, and check_frames the closest pairs, many frames at a time in C++, a frame on a thread, each as it would be alone: for the 105,464 frames of UNEP16 on an Apple M5, the NEP descriptors in 15 s rather than 26 s, its predictions in 24 s rather than 35 s, and the checks in 2 s rather than 8 s. novelty screens the pairs in float32 on threads and computes the distances of the candidates exactly: their novelty in 5 s rather than 30 s.

    Visualization¶

    • mdapy.visualization.render: pictures of systems by ray tracing, with ambient occlusion, shadows and outlines in four styles: fast, lit as the viewports of OVITO; matte, as the Tachyon renderer of OVITO (the default); shiny, a metallic sheen after its OSPRay renderer, with light bouncing between the atoms; flat, plain colors outlined as ASE draws atoms; colored by element, structure, values or by hand, with bonds, the box, Voronoi cells, surface meshes and dislocation lines with arrows of their Burgers vectors, cut at the periodic faces of the box; arrows of any vector of the atoms, such as displacements or forces, as OVITO's vector display draws them; polylines such as the paths of atoms; coordination polyhedra, the convex hulls of the neighbors of atoms with their volumes, as OVITO builds them; molecules as balls and sticks, licorice or space filling, with covalent bonds found from the elements; a Scene of spheres, cylinders and polygons; the scene framed, or a given focus and span; depth of field and soft shadows; every setting of a style can be changed, including the direction of the highlights, metals and lighting in sRGB; a million atoms in under 5 s on the processor in the matte style, or in under a second on the GPU, with Metal on macOS, Vulkan on Linux or Direct3D 12 on Windows, which use the ray tracing units of the GPU where it has them; show_image shows a picture in a notebook; legend=True says what the colors mean. See Rendering. A driver that runs on the processor does not count as a GPU.
    • Colors of atoms in mdapy.visualization: color_coding, color_by_type and assign_color color all atoms or a mask and keep the colors in the color column, which render draws by default, as OVITO's modifiers of these names; atom_colors and atom_radii with colors and radii of elements chosen and a scale; colormaps rainbow, viridis, magma, jet, hot, blue_white_red and gray computed as OVITO computes them, besides plasma and coolwarm, and labels for discrete values. Atom types without elements, as in a LAMMPS dump of types only, are drawn each in a color of its own, those of OVITO (TYPE_COLORS), and all of one radius; atom_names gives the names these colors go by, and read_trajectory(path, elements=[...]) names the types, so that files written afterwards hold the elements. See Coloring atoms.
    • mdapy.visualization.plot: matplotlib set up for journals, in notebooks too (widths of columns, Nature's artwork guide, Okabe–Ito colors, panel labels, saving as PDF, SVG and PNG) and plots of results: parity, g(r), S(q), bond angles, MSD with the diffusion coefficient, stacking fault energies, counts of structures, and two columns of the atoms against each other (scatter). See Plots.

    Interoperability¶

    • mdapy.interop.to_ase and from_ase convert to and from ase.Atoms, with velocities, charges, constraints, other numeric columns and the results of a calculation. See ASE.
    • mdapy.interop.to_ase_calculator makes any MDAPY calculator an ASE calculator, and from_ase_calculator any ASE calculator, such as that of torchnep, an MDAPY calculator. See ASE calculators.
    • mdapy.interop.to_ovito and from_ovito convert to and from OVITO data collections, with types, standard properties, the topology and the attributes, so that results of OVITO pipelines come back as columns. See OVITO.
    • mdapy.interop.to_pymatgen and from_pymatgen convert to and from pymatgen structures and molecules. See pymatgen.
    • mdapy.interop.to_freud gives the box and points of freud's analyses, for systems periodic in every direction, and from_freud takes them back. See freud.
    • Pages on exchanging files and calculators with LAMMPS, VASP and GPUMD.

    Performance¶

    • benchmarks/comparison.py times the analyses that MDAPY shares with OVITO and freud, on the same systems, where each program gives the result of MDAPY, each program in a process of its own with limits of memory and time. See MDAPY, OVITO and freud.
    • Extended XYZ files of many small frames read faster: the comment line is split at once in its common form, the element column comes from the categories kept for the file, and the box and the columns are checked with less work. The 105,464 frames of UNEP16 read in 15 s rather than 19 s on an Apple M5; small frames about 40 % faster.

    Tutorials¶

    • Learn MDAPY: six short lessons that run on their own, from a crystal as a table to a nanoparticle that melts and your own LAMMPS dumps, and a cheat sheet. See A crystal is a table.
    • Worked examples that combine several parts of MDAPY: a polycrystal of fcc and bcc grains, columnar grains on a hexagonal pattern with chosen misorientations, the defect energies of copper with checks of their convergence, when sharing neighbor lists pays, what a System shares and copies, a machine-learned potential for CrCoNi: the phonons of Cr, Co and Ni against DFT and the elastic constants of the alloy from 0 to 1000 K against measurements, a high-throughput screen of alloys in many processes with the thread settings it needs, and a movie that flies through a crystal.     Foundations¶

    • Released under the Mozilla Public License 2.0: MDAPY may be used in any software, open or closed, and changes to its own files are shared under the same license. MDAPY 1.x is under the GPL 3.0 or later.
    • Python 3.11 or newer; NumPy 2.3.1 or newer and polars 2 are the only required dependencies.
    • All compiled code is one extension module, built as a single abi3 wheel per platform for Python 3.12 and newer, optimized for speed.
    • Atom indices in results are 32-bit, mdapy.index_dtype; a build with -Ccmake.define.MDAPY_INDEX64=ON uses 64-bit indices for more than 2,147,483,647 atoms. See Installation.
    • A thread pool of MDAPY's own replaces OpenMP, so MDAPY can run in the same process as PyTorch and LAMMPS. Results are identical for any number of threads. The calling thread works too, idle threads wait for the next step briefly before they sleep, and a step of a few pieces wakes only as many threads, so small systems are not slowed down by many cores. See Parallelism.
    • mdapy.show_versions prints the versions of MDAPY, Python and the packages it depends on, and the processor, memory and GPU of the machine, to paste into an issue. The bug report form of the issue tracker asks for it and for the shortest code that shows the problem.
    • mdapy.set_num_threads, mdapy.get_num_threads, mdapy.num_threads and the MDAPY_NUM_THREADS environment variable control the number of threads. The default is the logical CPUs the process may use, so that a job of a cluster on part of a node starts no thread for the rest of it (108 s against 6 s for the same relaxations on 2 of 16 CPUs). A forked process gets a warning: polars hangs in it once its parent has used its threads, so start processes with spawn. See Parallelism.
    • mdapy.CancelToken, progress callbacks and Ctrl-C stop or follow long computations.
    • mdapy.visualization.write_movie writes pictures as a movie with the FFmpeg of the user, taking them one by one from a generator. See Movies.
    • render refuses pictures above 8192 pixels on a side or 16384 subpixels, and write_movie movies above 8192 × 4320 pixels, as larger ones can use up the memory or play nowhere.
    • mdapy.units defines every physical constant and conversion factor. See Units and conventions.
    • mdapy.errors defines the exception types. FileFormatError reports a malformed file with its name and line number.
    • Text files are parsed and written by compiled kernels in parallel. Numbers are read correctly rounded and written in the shortest form that reads back to the same value, whatever the locale of the process; unit conversions by powers of ten are exact.
    • macOS wheels need macOS 14 or newer.
    • mdapy.elements holds chemical symbols and IUPAC standard atomic weights, and identifies elements from masses. See Elements.

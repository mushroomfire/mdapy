    Defect energies of copper¶

    The energies of vacancies, surfaces and stacking faults are the first check of a potential for a metal. This page computes them for copper with the EAM potential of Zhou et al., and checks that each has converged with the size of the cell. See Defect energies for the functions themselves.

    The lattice constant¶

    Every cell below is built with the lattice constant of the potential, not the measured one: relax the cubic cell of fcc copper at zero pressure.

    import numpy as np
from mdapy.build import build_crystal
from mdapy.calculators import EAM
from mdapy.simulation import minimize

copper = EAM.zhou(["Cu"])
cell = build_crystal("fcc", 3.6, elements="Cu")
relaxed = minimize(cell, copper, cell="isotropic", max_stress=1e-6).system
a = relaxed.box.matrix[0, 0]
round(a, 4)  # 3.615 Å

    Vacancies¶

    A vacancy disturbs the atoms around it, and in a small periodic cell it also meets its own images. Compute the formation energy in cells of 2 to 5 cubic cells along each side, until it no longer changes:

    from mdapy.simulation import vacancy_formation_energy

for n in (2, 3, 4, 5):
    crystal = build_crystal("fcc", a, elements="Cu", repeat=(n, n, n))
    energy = vacancy_formation_energy(crystal, copper).energy
    print(f"{len(crystal):4d} atoms: {energy:.3f} eV")

      32 atoms: 1.301 eV
 108 atoms: 1.279 eV
 256 atoms: 1.275 eV
 500 atoms: 1.273 eV

    256 atoms are converged to some meV; the measured value is about 1.28 eV.

    Surfaces¶

    For each face, orient the crystal with the face normal to z, about 24 Å thick and 8 Å wide, and open it with vacuum along z:

    from mdapy.simulation import surface_energy

faces = {
    "(100)": [(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1)],
    "(110)": [(0, 0, 1), (1, -1, 0), (1, 1, 0)],
    "(111)": [(1, 1, -2), (-1, 1, 0), (1, 1, 1)],
}
for face, orientation in faces.items():
    unit = build_crystal("fcc", a, elements="Cu", orientation=orientation)
    lengths = np.linalg.norm(unit.box.matrix, axis=1)
    repeat = np.maximum(1, np.round([8.0, 8.0, 24.0] / lengths)).astype(int)
    slab = build_crystal(
        "fcc", a, elements="Cu", orientation=orientation, repeat=tuple(repeat)
    )
    print(f"{face}: {surface_energy(slab, copper).energy:.3f} J/m²")

    (100): 1.563 J/m²
(110): 1.743 J/m²
(111): 1.501 J/m²

    The close-packed (111) face costs least and the open (110) face most, as for every fcc metal.

    Stacking faults¶

    The generalized stacking fault energy is the energy of slipping the upper half of a crystal over the lower half on a (111) plane. Along [11-2] it has a local minimum at a third of the period, a/6 [11-2], the intrinsic stacking fault, behind the unstable stacking fault energy.

    The atoms relax only along the normal of the fault, and how much room they have for that depends on the cell. Compare the intrinsic stacking fault in crystals periodic along the normal and in slabs between two free surfaces, whose energy cancels in the difference, for 9 to 36 (111) planes:

    import mdapy as mp
from mdapy.simulation import stacking_fault_energy

partial_period = [a * np.sqrt(6) / 2, 0, 0]  # [11-2]
for n in (3, 6, 9, 12):
    periodic = build_crystal(
        "fcc", a, elements="Cu", orientation=faces["(111)"], repeat=(1, 2, n)
    )
    slab = periodic.with_box(mp.Box(periodic.box.matrix, pbc=[True, True, False]))
    energies = [
        stacking_fault_energy(
            crystal, copper, partial_period, points=4, max_force=1e-6
        ).energy[1]
        * 1000
        for crystal in (periodic, slab)
    ]
    print(f"{3 * n:2d} planes: {energies[0]:5.2f} and {energies[1]:5.2f} mJ/m²")

     9 planes: 27.27 and 22.62 mJ/m²
18 planes: 24.46 and 22.46 mJ/m²
27 planes: 23.32 and 22.46 mJ/m²
36 planes: 22.60 and 22.46 mJ/m²

    Between free surfaces the energy has converged at 18 planes. In a periodic crystal the height of the box stays as it is: the planes next to the fault move apart and squeeze the others, which costs an energy that fades only slowly with the thickness. Use slabs, and a tight max_force: with the default of 1e-4 eV/Å the energies scatter by some tenths of a mJ/m².

    Then slip along both directions, with 24 steps per period:

    layers = build_crystal(
    "fcc", a, elements="Cu", orientation=faces["(111)"], repeat=(1, 2, 6)
)
slab = layers.with_box(mp.Box(layers.box.matrix, pbc=[True, True, False]))
partial = stacking_fault_energy(slab, copper, partial_period, points=25, max_force=1e-6)
full = stacking_fault_energy(
    slab, copper, [0, a * np.sqrt(2) / 2, 0], points=25, max_force=1e-6
)
intrinsic = partial.energy[8] * 1000  # mJ/m², at 8/24 of the period
unstable = partial.energy[:9].max() * 1000
round(intrinsic, 1), round(unstable, 1)  # (22.5, 110.5)

    The measured intrinsic stacking fault energy of copper is about 40 to 45 mJ/m²; this potential gives about half of it, which matters for anything that involves partial dislocations.

    The curves¶

    from mdapy.visualization import plot

with plot.style():
    fig, ax = plot.figure("single")
    ax.plot(partial.fraction, partial.energy * 1000, "o-", label="[11-2]")
    ax.plot(full.fraction, full.energy * 1000, "s-", label="[-110]")
    ax.set_xlabel("slip / period")
    ax.set_ylabel("energy (mJ/m²)")
    ax.legend(frameon=False)
    plot.save(fig, "images/gsfe", formats=("png",))
